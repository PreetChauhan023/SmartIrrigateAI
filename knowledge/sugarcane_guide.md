# Sugarcane irrigation and fertigation quick guide (demo knowledge base)

## Growth stages and water need
Germination (0-3 months): keep soil moist but not waterlogged; light, frequent irrigation every 7-10 days helps uniform sprouting.
Tillering (3-6 months): moderate water need; irrigate every 10-12 days depending on soil type. Water stress here reduces the number of tillers.
Grand growth (6-10 months): highest water demand. Do not allow moisture stress; irrigate every 7-10 days in summer. Moisture stress in this stage causes the largest yield loss.
Maturity / ripening (10-12 months): reduce irrigation 3-4 weeks before harvest to increase sugar content.

## Irrigation timing
Irrigate in early morning or evening to reduce evaporation losses.
Skip irrigation when heavy rainfall is expected within 24 hours, then re-check soil moisture.
Drip irrigation can save 30-40 percent water compared with flood irrigation.
Avoid waterlogging; it damages roots and wastes electricity and water.

## Fertigation
Apply fertilizer through the drip system after irrigation has started, not before heavy rain.
Nitrogen (urea) is applied in split doses during tillering and grand growth.
Potash (MOP) is important during grand growth for stalk weight and sugar.
Flush the drip lines with clean water after fertigation.

## Pump and electricity
Schedule pumping during hours when power supply is available; many villages get agricultural power in fixed windows.
Check pump and filters regularly; a clogged filter reduces water delivered per hour.

## Signs of water stress
Leaves roll and fold in the afternoon, leaf tips dry, and canopy growth slows. Irrigate soon and check soil moisture with a sensor or by hand.
